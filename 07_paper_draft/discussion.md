## 7. Discussion

**What the rules do.** The out-of-scope gate supplies the refusals: out-of-scope questions fall into a few recurring kinds (file status, complaints, penalties, police internal matters) worded regularly enough for patterns, and classifiers trained on the same labels were not significantly better in balanced or end-to-end accuracy on either test set. The citizen lexicon supplies the answers by mapping citizen wording to official names. The reranker favors complaints that name a real procedure, and the LLM reader labels many long procedural narratives as complaints.

**What remains.** Of the 24 *answer* questions that A misses on the first test set (*post hoc* analysis), 12 are refused, 4 of them by the gate although the base system had ranked the correct procedure first; of the other 12, 7 have the correct procedure outside the top five, typically in long letters that only imply it. With such low answer precision on random traffic, an assistant should have a human officer confirm its answers, or answer only when confident[11]; refusing better is necessary but not sufficient.

**Why the second round did not carry over.** A2 + B changes the decision or procedure of a similar share of questions on development and on the new set (6.2% and 5.2%), but with 30 gains and no losses on development against 4 and 4 on the new set (a decision counting as correct only with the right procedure); its new out-of-scope patterns fixed 15 wrong answers on development but one on the new set, which suggests that they fit particular phrasings. The small *answer* class and labels from another model with a slightly extended guideline may also contribute.

**Limitations and threats to validity.** (i) One domain, language and data snapshot: hand-written rules for Vietnamese commune-level procedures, with no claim about provincial or ministry procedures. (ii) With only 59 and 21 *answer* and 6 and 7 *clarify* test questions, small differences are not significant and nothing can be concluded about clarification. (iii) The development and first-test labels were produced with the AI assistant that wrote the rules, so the gate may encode its notion of scope; the second-pass sample and the second test set were labeled with another model and checked by the project lead, who had seen aggregate class proportions and knew the kinds of rules being added. Circularity alone does not explain the second test set, whose labels did not come from that assistant (though they follow the same guideline) yet where the rule layer still gains 125 questions and loses 5 on behavior (122 more correct refusals). No fully independent human annotation exists, however. (iv) All questions come from two portals, with a larger gain on one, and the 79% refusals of our samples understate the share in random traffic. (v) The locks are commits in our own repository, without an external timestamp. (vi) The rules received far more development effort than the learned components and the RAG baseline, systems from other studies could not be run on our questions, and the ablations and learned gates are *post hoc*. (vii) LLM components ran once at temperature 0 on one quantized model.
