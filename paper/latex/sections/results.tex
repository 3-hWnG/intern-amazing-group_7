\subsection{Team-written versus real questions}

On the same frozen base system (Table~\ref{tab:gap}), behavior accuracy drops by about 21 points from team-written to real questions,
balanced accuracy by about 0.2 and top-1 accuracy by 38 to 59 points. The largest change is in what is asked: 78--79\% of real questions
should be refused, against 11\% of team-written ones, and the base system refuses only about half of them. On the team-written set the rule
layer makes no net change in behavior ($+4/-4$, 400/518 in both cases; correct refusals 52 to 55 of 58), so a team-written set alone
would have shown neither the problem nor the fix.

\subsection{First test set}\label{sec:test1}

The rule layer (Table~\ref{tab:test}) raises balanced accuracy from 0.647 to 0.814 and end-to-end accuracy from 19/59 (95\% interval
22--45\%) to 35/59 (47--71\%); base $\rightarrow$ A gains 96 and loses 10 questions on behavior ($p\approx 9\cdot10^{-19}$) and 22 against 6
end-to-end ($p=0.004$). Correct refusals rise from 132 to 219 of 248 while correct \emph{answer} decisions stay level (45 to 44). Without
the 11 near-duplicates of development questions the picture is the same (302 questions: balanced accuracy 0.651 to 0.810, end-to-end 19 to
34 of 58, $+92/-10$, $p\approx 9\cdot10^{-18}$). Used alone, the reranker (0.692) and the LLM reader (0.785) help less. Added to the rules,
the reranker gains one question ($+1/-0$; the selected configuration, 0.823 and 36/59). The reader's intents add refusals ($+25/-10$ on
behavior against A, $p=0.017$) but lose answers (32/59; $+8/-11$ end-to-end, $p=0.65$); its procedure name alone gives the highest scores
(0.834, 39/59), but this configuration was not selected on development and its end-to-end gain over A is not significant ($+8/-4$,
$p=0.39$). The RAG baseline in which the model decides has the lowest balanced accuracy, 0.489, below always refusing: it asks for
clarification on 118 of the 248 questions it should refuse (against A: $+16/-141$ on behavior, $p\approx 4\cdot10^{-26}$; end-to-end 28
against 35, $p=0.23$). No configuration recognizes more than one of the six \emph{clarify} questions.

\subsection{Second test set}

On the new questions (Table~\ref{tab:v4}) the rule layer again changes refusals most: correct refusals rise from 175 to 297 of 335 and
balanced accuracy from 0.571 to 0.705 (base $\rightarrow$ A: $+125/-5$, $p\approx 4\cdot10^{-31}$). The second round does not carry over.
A2 + B, which had gained 25 questions on behavior and lost none against A on development, ties A here ($+4/-4$, $p=1$). In the comparisons
declared before the run, it ties the first-round selection A + B on behavior ($+3/-3$, $p=1$; end-to-end 10 against 8 of 21, $+3/-1$,
$p=0.63$) and beats the base ($+129/-9$, $p\approx 2\cdot10^{-28}$; end-to-end 10 against 5, $+7/-2$, $p=0.18$). With 21 answerable
questions, end-to-end intervals are wide (A: 9/21, 24--63\%; A2 + B: 10/21, 28--68\%). R is again last (0.413; $+19/-122$ on behavior
against A2 + B), although it recognizes 4 of the 7 \emph{clarify} questions, against at most one for the other configurations.
