An assistant for the procedures of commune and ward offices must decide whether to answer, clarify, or refuse. We study it on
real citizen questions from two Vietnamese public-service portals, with locked held-out sets and one run per configuration; a second label pass on 100 questions gives $\kappa=0.685$. On one frozen system, behavior accuracy falls from 77.2\%
on team-written questions to 56.5\% on real ones, 79\% of which must be refused. A transparent rule layer
raises balanced accuracy from 0.647 to 0.814 and end-to-end answers from 19 to 35 of 59, with no net change on
the team-written set; on a second locked set it raises correct refusals from 175 to 297 of 335. A reranker, a local 4-billion-parameter
LLM and a second round of rules add no significant end-to-end gain, and a retrieval-augmented baseline where the model decides scores
below always refusing.
